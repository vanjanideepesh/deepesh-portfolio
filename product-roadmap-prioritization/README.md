# Product Roadmap & Backlog Prioritization

**How I turned founder interviews and competitive research into a MoSCoW-prioritized backlog for a music-tech distribution MVP — and why the sequencing decisions mattered as much as the feature list itself.**

---

## The context

Building a distribution platform for independent artists in the Indian music-tech space, working with a 6-member product & engineering team toward two sequential GTM launches. The hard part was never "what could we build" — it was deciding, with limited engineering capacity, **what had to be true before we asked artists to trust us with their income.**

## The inputs

- Direct interviews with 40+ independent artists, surfacing pain points with existing manual distribution workflows
- Competitive analysis against three existing distribution platforms, to identify table-stakes vs. differentiators
- Early stakeholder conversations with two potential label partners, surfacing a hard requirement (bulk catalog upload) we hadn't originally scoped
- An internal engineering feasibility pass on effort vs. impact for each candidate feature

## The prioritization

See [`artifacts/backlog_moscow.csv`](./artifacts/backlog_moscow.csv) for the full backlog with MoSCoW category and the specific research input behind each call.

The single decision I'd highlight over any other: **royalty tracking was a "Must," not a "Should,"** even though it added real time to the schedule. It would have been easy to treat it as a fast-follow — the core upload-and-distribute flow is the more obvious MVP. But user interviews were consistent: trust in payment accuracy was the single biggest churn risk for this specific audience, and losing early-adopter trust before Launch 1 would have been far more expensive to repair than the extra sprint cost to build it right the first time.

See [`artifacts/roadmap_timeline.md`](./artifacts/roadmap_timeline.md) for the full sequencing, including why the team shipped toward **two** launches instead of one.

## Outcome

Shipped to TRL-5 with 10+ paying customers and $125K in annual revenue; the two-launch structure and MoSCoW-driven backlog contributed to a 30% reduction in time-to-market versus the team's original single-launch estimate, by making early trade-off calls explicit instead of re-litigating scope mid-sprint.

## What I'd do differently

I'd formalize the "research input" column from day one rather than reconstructing it retroactively — in the moment, prioritization rationale lived in my head and in Slack threads. Making the evidence-to-decision link explicit and written down earlier would have made it much easier to revisit decisions later as new data came in, instead of re-deriving the reasoning from memory.
